Stand up. The drawing copies you.

Your webcam finds thirty-three points on your body, and we turn those into the joint angles the paper figure runs on. It isn't tracing your picture — it's measuring how you're bent and bending the drawing the same way.

Where your video goes

Nowhere. The frames go from your camera into the pose model inside this tab and are thrown away. There is no upload, no server, and no recording — the page has no way to send a picture of you anywhere, and the camera stops the moment you switch tabs. The only thing that ever exists past a frame is a short list of angles.

How it works, honestly

  1. Nothing here is trained. Pose estimation is a solved problem; this uses Google's BlazePose, pretrained and free, running on your GPU in this tab. Training one from scratch would cost thousands of GPU-hours to end up worse.
  2. The model returns 33 landmarks per frame — shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles and the rest, as coordinates.
  3. Retargeting is the actual work, and it is one line per limb: atan2(child.x − parent.x, child.y − parent.y). Knees and elbows are measured relative to the thigh and upper arm, which is what makes them behave like joints instead of free hinges.
  4. Everything is scaled by torso length, so standing close to the camera or far away drives the drawing exactly the same.
  5. Smoothing, or it vibrates. Raw landmarks jitter a few pixels every frame. Angles are eased toward their new value, taking the short way round so they never spin the long way.
  6. The same rig draws both. The battle's figures and this one share one drawing function. The only difference is where the angles come from — invented by a gait function there, measured off you here.
