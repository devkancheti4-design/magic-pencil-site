Liber bestiarum · of dragons

The Dragons of the War

Of the dragons there is more set down than of the men who drew them. A dragon is a drawing, and a drawing may be copied, and these six leaves were copied a great many times before they came into this book. Four of the six were drawn with his pencil. Two were drawn with theirs. Not all were named dragon — the name given is what the thing does, and a wyrm was named wyrm — but the field-scribes filed them together, and so does this book. The measurements are theirs. The tally runs to ten and no further; ten is what a scribe writes down when a thing is past counting.

The plates stir when the eye rests on them, or when the reader asks.

  1. cap. iii · at the crease

    The Fire Drake

    Draco ignifer communis

    Iries drew the first of these at Creasemarch on the third day, standing on ground he had taken that morning, and named it dragon. It burned the roach pontoons on both banks before the ink was dry, and the fold-line held until evening. This plate is copied from his own leaf, which is why the near wing is larger than the far one. He drew it in a hurry.

    Wingspan
    nine paces, tip to tip
    Breath
    a barn's length of flame
    Temper
    hot, and answers to its name

  2. cap. ii · the turned corners

    The Paper Wyrm

    Serpens plicatilis

    It has no legs and it does not fly. It runs along a fold the way rain runs along a gutter, and Dogear Downs is nothing but folds. He let four into the Downs at dusk, on the eve of the retaking; by morning every turned corner had been pressed flat and the roach garrisons under them with it. He named it wyrm, and a wyrm is what it did.

    Wingspan
    none; it has no wings
    Breath
    a cold seam, and no fire
    Temper
    patient past reason

  3. cap. v · drawn by the roaches

    The Ink Drake

    Draco atramentarius

    The roaches drew this one, and named it well. It does not burn; it floods. What it breathed upon stayed breathed upon. Nineteen of his own drawings went into the spill at Blotmire and none came out again. Iries ended it on the fourth morning with a sponge, drawn small and named plainly. The scribe sets this down without comment.

    A later hand has written beside this plate: their nineteen names are on the facing leaf, and I have counted them twice.

    Wingspan
    four paces, and mostly for show
    Breath
    a flood, not a flame
    Temper
    obedient to the roach hand

  4. cap. iv · over the erasure

    The Cloud Serpent

    Serpens nubifer

    It is feathered, and it drifts rather than flies. It carries no fire and was never once set against a living thing. These were drawn on held ground at Creasemarch and flown east over Smudgewood, where nothing keeps its edge and a drawn creature may come apart in the air. They kept their own outlines for eleven days, and carried the rest of his work out over the erasure.

    Wingspan
    seven paces of feather
    Breath
    mist, and only mist
    Temper
    mild; it will not strike

  5. cap. v · the granary

    The Ember Hatchling

    Draco minimus molestus

    A dragon drawn small, or drawn in the dark, or drawn with the hand shaking. It cannot carry a rider or break a gate; the shape is not there for it. It can get inside a granary. One did, on the Blotmire causeway, and the roaches spent four days of that siege chasing it round the sacks instead of holding the road. The plate is to the same scale as the others.

    Wingspan
    a handspan, at most
    Breath
    a cupful; enough for straw
    Temper
    past all bearing

  6. cap. vi · at the throne

    The Crayon Titan

    Draco maximus scriptorius

    The roaches drew it once, before their own throne, on the last day, and the drawing took four hours. The shape decides the strength, and there had never been so much shape on one leaf. It went through the gate-house without appearing to notice the gate-house. When Iries broke their pencil it stopped where it stood, and it stands there yet; nothing since has been drawn large enough to move it.

    A later hand, and a shakier one: it is still there. I have walked round it. It took the better part of a morning.

    Wingspan
    twenty paces, and never opened
    Breath
    wax, and black smoke
    Temper
    slow to begin, slower to stop
